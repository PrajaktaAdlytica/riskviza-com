# Riskviza / Riskra Reference Board

Date: 2026-07-08  
Purpose: brainstorming and website reference set for Riskviza.com, the public website for Riskra.

## What We Are Looking For

Riskra needs a website that feels credible to risk managers, compliance teams, insurers, and boards. The reference set should not copy one site directly. It should combine:

- Enterprise-grade trust from GRC and risk platforms.
- Modern SaaS clarity from compliance automation platforms.
- Vendor-risk specificity from TPRM platforms.
- EU regulatory seriousness from NIS2, DORA, CSRD, and AI governance tooling.
- Board-level storytelling through clear reporting visuals.

## Primary Reference Websites

## User Reaction Summary

Latest direction from screenshot review:

- MetricStream is too text-heavy. Use it only for category credibility, not visual style.
- Archer is also too text-heavy and does not feel trustworthy enough visually. Avoid this style.
- OneTrust has a strong hero, good grid structure, useful visuals inside the grid, and subtle hover potential.
- Bitsight is acceptable but not a lead visual reference.
- Panorays has useful dashboard designs and vendor-risk product visuals.
- Vanta is the strongest reference overall, especially the automated-compliance page, feature sections, scrolling rhythm, varied backgrounds, polished UI screenshots, and animated framework-style visuals.
- Hyperproof feels too loud and less trustworthy. Do not use it as a core visual reference.
- LogicGate is decent, especially the problem framing section.
- SureCloud's "Your GRC programme has two problems" section is liked. Use the pattern, but improve the visual tone for Riskra.
- DORA/Resiplan and Orbiq were not liked visually. Keep only regulatory specificity, not design style.

Design translation:

- Replace long text with bar charts, line graphs, pie charts, donut charts, scorecards, statistics, status indicators, and product UI.
- Use subtle hover effects on cards, grids, tabs, buttons, and dashboard modules.
- Use strong realistic screenshots and UI composites.
- Build an animated/scroll-reactive framework visual inspired by Vanta's "Every framework you need" section.
- Use different section backgrounds to keep the page premium and alive.
- Avoid dense copy, generic enterprise GRC layouts, and old-school compliance visuals.

### 1. MetricStream

URL: https://www.metricstream.com/integrated-risk-management.html

Why it matters:

MetricStream is a classic enterprise GRC reference. It shows how large vendors frame integrated risk management, multiple risk categories, controls, compliance, audit, and board reporting. It is not a preferred visual direction.

Borrow:

- Risk category breadth.
- Executive and board-level language.
- Modular product IA.

Avoid:

- Text-heavy enterprise tone.
- Dense pages that feel hard to scan.
- Generic "single source of truth" language without sharper EU positioning.
- Layouts where copy does most of the work instead of charts, screenshots, and product visuals.

### 2. Archer

URL: https://www.archerirm.com/about-us

Why it matters:

Archer is a mature integrated risk management platform. It is useful for understanding what buyers expect from established GRC vendors: risk, compliance, audit, operational resilience, and enterprise control.

Borrow:

- Enterprise risk language.
- Serious, regulated-industry trust.

Avoid:

- Looking too legacy.
- Overly abstract platform claims.
- Complex navigation that makes the product feel slow to adopt.
- Overly empty or bland visuals that do not build trust.

### 6. Vanta Automated Compliance

## Design Patterns To Borrow

### Pattern A: Board-Ready First Screen

Use a first viewport that immediately says this is for serious risk decisions, not only compliance task tracking.

Suggested treatment:

- Left: headline and CTA.
- Right: realistic product interface showing board-risk summary, top risks, overdue evidence, vendor exposure, and regulatory mapping.
- Small trust row: EU-native, NIS2/DORA/AI Act/CSRD/GDPR aligned, built for risk and compliance teams.

### Pattern B: Three-Product Platform Story

Riskra has a clean product architecture:

- Register: where risks live.
- Evidence: where proof lives.
- Vendors: where dependencies live.

The homepage should use these three modules as the spine of the page.

### Pattern C: Regulation Matrix Without Looking Bureaucratic

Show regulations as connected obligations, not a wall of badges.

Suggested visual:

- Rows: NIS2, DORA, EU AI Act, CSRD/ESRS, GDPR.
- Columns: risk register, evidence, vendor oversight, board reporting.
- Use subtle checkmarks or status dots.

### Pattern C2: Animated Framework Mapping Visual

Inspired by Vanta's "Every framework you need" section.

Riskra version:

- Headline: "Map one control across NIS2, DORA, AI Act, CSRD, and GDPR."
- Left side: large statistic such as "74% evidence reuse" or "5 frameworks, one control library."
- Right side: animated product visual with control cards, framework chips, progress bars, donut overlap, and a moving dotted path.
- Bottom chips: Risk Register, Vendor Evidence, AI Controls, Board Pack, Audit Trail.
- Scroll behavior: as the user scrolls, framework chips activate and the product visual updates.

### Pattern D: Evidence Chain

Riskra Evidence needs its own visual metaphor:

Risk -> Control -> Owner -> Evidence -> Review -> Board Finding.

This is more distinctive than a generic file repository.

### Pattern E: Vendor Dependency Map

Riskra Vendors should show:

- Critical vendors.
- ICT providers.
- AI providers.
- ESG/supply-chain flags.
- Contract and evidence status.
- Risk contribution to board-level exposure.

This gives the product a strong visual signature.

### Pattern F: Board Pack Preview

Most competitors mention dashboards. Riskra should show board-ready reporting more concretely.

Suggested visuals:

- Quarterly board risk pack.
- "Decision needed" callout.
- Scenario summary.
- Evidence confidence.
- Risk trend and remediation status.

### Pattern G: Interactive Tabbed Pain-Point Section

Inspired by SureCloud's "Your GRC programme has two problems."

This section type is called:

- Interactive tabbed pain-point section.
- Segmented problem explorer.
- Problem-tabs section.

Riskra version:

Headline: "Risk teams do not have one risk problem. They have five disconnected views."

Tabs:

- Disconnected risks, slow decisions.
- Evidence lives everywhere.
- Vendors change faster than reviews.
- AI risk is invisible to the board.
- ESG and operational risk stay separate.

Each active tab should show:

- One concise pain statement.
- One visual, preferably a product UI mockup, chart, graph, or evidence trail.
- One "Riskra fixes this by..." outcome.

Avoid:

- Stock photos as the main visual.
- Long paragraphs.
- Overdramatic copy.

### Pattern H: Chart-Led Trust Sections

Replace text-heavy explanation with:

- Bar charts for risk by domain.
- Donut charts for evidence completion.
- Line charts for risk trend over time.
- Pie/donut overlap for framework reuse.
- Scorecards for vendor criticality and assurance confidence.
- Small statistics blocks for time saved, evidence reuse, vendor review status, and board-pack readiness.

## Design Directions To Explore

### Direction 1: EU Risk Command Center

Feel:

Calm, premium, structured, boardroom-grade.

Visual language:

Light interface, deep ink typography, cool neutral background, green/teal signals, precise charts, bar charts, donut charts, low-radius UI, strong whitespace.

Best for:

Boards, insurers, regulated risk teams.

Risk:

Could feel too conservative if product visuals are not strong.

### Direction 2: Evidence-Backed Trust Layer

Feel:

Modern compliance automation meets audit assurance.

Visual language:

Light SaaS layout, evidence cards, control status, document trails, trust indicators, product-led storytelling.

Best for:

Riskra Evidence and compliance teams.

Risk:

Could feel too much like SOC 2/compliance automation unless EU and board reporting are prominent.

### Direction 3: Connected Risk Graph

Feel:

More distinctive, strategic, and future-facing.

Visual language:

Risk network map connecting vendors, AI systems, ESG topics, cyber controls, incidents, regulations, and board decisions.

Best for:

Differentiating Riskra from generic GRC platforms.

Risk:

Needs careful execution so it does not become abstract decoration.

## Recommended Blend

The strongest route is a blend of:

- Vanta as the primary page rhythm and interaction benchmark.
- OneTrust as the hero/grid inspiration.
- Panorays as the dashboard/product-screenshot inspiration.
- SureCloud's tabbed problem explorer, redesigned in Riskra's premium visual language.
- Direction 1 for the homepage hero and brand tone.
- Direction 3 for the connected risk metaphor.
- Direction 2 for Riskra Evidence details.

This gives Riskra the maturity of enterprise GRC, the clarity of modern SaaS, and a unique product story around connected, evidence-backed board decisions.

## What Not To Copy

- Generic blue cybersecurity gradients.
- Shield/lock overload.
- Dense enterprise copy blocks.
- Abstract "AI-powered" claims without explainability.
- A hero with no product screenshot.
- Regulation badge walls with no product implication.
- Template-style SaaS cards with weak hierarchy.

## Immediate Creative Requirements For Riskra

- Create a real logo and favicon before building.
- Use a premium light theme.
- Use real-looking product screenshots or generated UI composites.
- Make board reporting visible above the fold.
- Give each product module a distinct but connected visual.
- Make the EU-native angle unmistakable but not bureaucratic.
- Keep typography and spacing sharper than a typical compliance website.
- Keep text short and visual-first.
- Use charts, graphs, status cards, and statistics instead of long paragraphs.
- Add subtle hover effects to grids, cards, tabs, CTAs, and product modules.
- Build at least one animated/scroll-reactive visual inspired by Vanta's framework mapping section.
- Use varied section backgrounds to create momentum without making the site loud.
- Avoid MetricStream/Archer-style text density.
- Avoid Hyperproof-style loudness.
- Avoid DORA/Orbiq visual styling.
