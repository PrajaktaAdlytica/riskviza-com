# Riskviza / Riskra Research Brief

Date: 2026-07-08  
Scope: public website strategy research for Riskviza.com, the future website for Riskra.

## Product Starting Point

Riskra is a Poland/EU-based product startup for risk managers, compliance teams, insurers, and boards.

Core problem:

Cyber, vendor, AI, ESG, and operational risks are tracked separately, making board-level decisions slow, incomplete, and hard to defend.

Core products:

- Riskra Register: unified risk register across cyber, vendor, AI, ESG, and operational risk.
- Riskra Evidence: evidence, assurance, control, and audit-readiness layer.
- Riskra Vendors: third-party and ICT/vendor risk management.

## Executive Read

Riskra should not position as just another GRC platform. The stronger market story is an EU-native risk operating layer for organizations that have outgrown spreadsheets and point tools but do not want global-enterprise GRC complexity.

The regulatory environment supports this clearly. NIS2 pushes cybersecurity and supply-chain risk into management accountability. DORA makes ICT risk, incident reporting, resilience testing, and ICT third-party oversight central for financial entities and insurers. The EU AI Act creates lifecycle AI risk management needs. CSRD/ESRS makes ESG risk, double materiality, evidence, and governance part of board-level risk work. GDPR remains a cross-cutting data protection and vendor accountability driver.

The website should lead with board-ready, evidence-backed decision-making, not compliance checklists. Competitors already claim "unified platform" and "AI-powered GRC," but most sound global, enterprise-heavy, and generic. Riskra can own a sharper position: built for EU-regulated mid-market teams that need cyber, vendor, AI, ESG, and operational risk connected in one board-facing view.

## Highest-Signal User Problems

### 1. Risk Data Is Split Across Teams And Tools

User goal: understand top risks without chasing five functions.

Surface: risk register, board pack, audit pack, executive reporting.

What breaks: cyber risk, vendor exposure, AI systems, ESG issues, incidents, and controls often live in separate spreadsheets, security tools, procurement files, ESG systems, and slide decks.

Severity: high.  
Frequency signal: high across GRC/IRM market messaging and EU regulatory overlap.  
Confidence: high.

Recommended product move: position Riskra Register as the shared spine: one risk model, many domains, with links to vendors, controls, obligations, evidence, incidents, and board reports.

### 2. Vendor And ICT Third-Party Risk Is Becoming Board-Level

User goal: know which providers create business, cyber, operational, AI, or ESG exposure.

Surface: vendor inventory, ICT provider register, questionnaires, contract evidence, ongoing monitoring.

What breaks: supplier risk is often managed at onboarding, then forgotten until an incident, renewal, audit, or regulatory request.

Severity: high.  
Frequency signal: high, especially with DORA and NIS2.  
Confidence: high.

Recommended product move: make Riskra Vendors feel like more than a vendor list. It should show dependency mapping, supplier risk scoring, ICT criticality, AI usage, ESG exposure, ownership, evidence, and remediation status.

### 3. Evidence Is The Hidden Bottleneck

User goal: prove risk management is operating, not merely documented.

Surface: evidence repository, audit readiness, control testing, regulatory responses, board assurance.

What breaks: teams can name the policy or control, but evidence sits in email, shared drives, screenshots, logs, PDFs, meeting notes, or external systems.

Severity: high.  
Frequency signal: medium-high from DORA, NIS2, AI Act, CSRD, and GRC platform patterns.  
Confidence: high.

Recommended product move: make Riskra Evidence a first-class product, not a small "attachments" feature. Message it as evidence-linked risk decisions and regulator-ready assurance.

### 4. Boards Need Decision-Ready Narratives, Not More Dashboards

User goal: see what changed, what matters, what decision is needed, and what evidence supports it.

Surface: board reporting, risk committee packs, crisis briefings, investor updates.

What breaks: dashboards show status, but boards still need a coherent narrative across risk domains, ownership, exposure, financial/business impact, and next actions.

Severity: high.  
Frequency signal: medium-high. Competitors mention board reporting, but often as a feature rather than the central product story.  
Confidence: medium-high.

Recommended product move: put "board-ready risk reporting" in the hero and product story. Use example board-pack visuals, scenario summaries, and evidence-linked findings.

### 5. AI Risk Is Both A New Domain And A Governance Trust Issue

User goal: identify AI systems, vendors, models, use cases, risk classes, controls, and evidence.

Surface: AI inventory, EU AI Act mapping, vendor AI usage, model risk reviews, approval workflows.

What breaks: many platforms talk about AI automation, but fewer make AI itself a governed risk domain with transparent evidence and oversight.

Severity: medium-high and rising.  
Frequency signal: rising due to the EU AI Act.  
Confidence: medium.

Recommended product move: present AI as a risk domain from the start: "AI systems, AI vendors, and AI evidence connected to your broader risk register."

## Regulatory Drivers To Use In Website Messaging

### NIS2

NIS2 expands cybersecurity requirements across essential and important EU sectors and emphasizes cybersecurity risk management, incident reporting, supervision, and supply-chain security. Official EU material notes policies for supply-chain security and vulnerability management as part of the NIS2 framework.

Website implication: "Cyber and supplier risk connected for NIS2-ready governance."

Source: [European Commission: NIS2 Directive](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/nis2-directive)

### DORA

DORA applies to the EU financial sector and focuses on ICT risk management, incident reporting, digital operational resilience testing, ICT third-party risk, and oversight of critical ICT third-party providers.

Website implication: make insurers and financial entities feel directly addressed. Show ICT provider registers, resilience evidence, incident timelines, and board reporting.

Sources: [EIOPA: DORA](https://www.eiopa.europa.eu/digital-operational-resilience-act-dora_en), [EIOPA: DORA Oversight](https://www.eiopa.europa.eu/digital-operational-resilience-act-dora/dora-oversight_en)

### EU AI Act

The EU AI Act establishes risk-based rules for AI developers and deployers, with strict obligations for high-risk AI systems including risk assessment, mitigation, documentation, transparency, monitoring, and human oversight.

Website implication: include AI risk inventory, AI system register, AI vendor exposure, and evidence-linked governance as a major section.

Source: [European Commission: AI Act regulatory framework](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)

### CSRD / ESRS

Companies in scope of CSRD must report according to European Sustainability Reporting Standards. The CSRD incorporates double materiality: companies report both how sustainability issues create financial risks and how company activities impact people and the environment.

Website implication: ESG should be connected to enterprise risk, vendor risk, evidence, and board oversight, rather than treated as a separate reporting island.

Sources: [European Commission: Corporate sustainability reporting](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/financial-markets/company-reporting-and-auditing/company-reporting/corporate-sustainability-reporting_en), [European Commission: double materiality summary](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/fisma/items/754701/en)

### GDPR

GDPR remains a cross-cutting risk driver for data protection, vendors/processors, security, breach response, and AI/data processing. For Riskra, GDPR should support the vendor, evidence, cyber, and AI story rather than dominate the positioning.

Website implication: show GDPR as an integrated obligation across vendors, incidents, evidence, and AI/data workflows.

Source: [European Commission: Data protection](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection_en)

## Brand Direction From Research

The brand should feel:

- EU-native and serious, but not bureaucratic.
- Premium SaaS, light, structured, and calm.
- Trustworthy enough for boards and insurers.
- Modern enough for AI/cyber risk.
- More "risk operating system" than "consulting brochure."

Avoid:

- Generic blue compliance template.
- Heavy enterprise-GRC clutter.
- Overuse of shields, locks, and cyber cliches.
- Abstract claims without product visuals.

Recommended visual motifs:

- Connected risk graph.
- Board pack/report preview.
- Evidence chain.
- Vendor dependency map.
- Regulatory alignment matrix.
